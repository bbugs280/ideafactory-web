## Post 1: The Problem (Tuesday)

**Headline:** Your deadlift is going up. Your bench is going up. Your body fat is going… nowhere. Here's why.

You train 4-5x a week. You progressive overload. You track every set. But when someone asks "what are your macros?" — you guess.

The truth: training is 20% of the result. Nutrition is 80%.

But most food trackers are built for dieters, not lifters. They don't care if you're bulking or cutting. They want you to weigh your chicken breast. They want your email and your data.

I got tired of it. So I built my own.

GainCut: snap a photo of your meal → AI identifies every food → macros calculated → logged against a target that knows whether you're gaining or cutting.

- Phase-aware: surplus for bulks, deficit for cuts
- Protein prioritized (higher during cuts to preserve muscle)
- Apple Health syncs your weight automatically
- No accounts. No tracking. Your data stays on your phone.

Built in Hong Kong 🇭🇰. Solo maker. Free on the App Store.

## Post 2: The Build (Thursday — follow-up)

48 hours after launching GainCut. Some real numbers:

- Built in 3 weeks (SwiftUI + Vision + Core ML)
- 167 foods in the recognition model
- 0 third-party analytics. Privacy-first by design.
- 1 person team. No co-founder. No funding.

The hardest part wasn't the AI — it was the nutrition logic. Phase-aware macro targets (bulk vs cut vs maintain) that adapt as your body weight changes. That took 4 rewrites.

What I'd do differently:
1. Start with a single food type (protein only) — faster MVP
2. Launch earlier — I waited until the UI was "polished enough"
3. Product Hunt page should go up BEFORE the app ships

If you're building something solo: ship before you're ready. The market will tell you what matters.

## Post 3: The Philosophy (following Tuesday)

IdeaFactory is a product studio with a rule: we score ideas before building them.

GainCut scored 42/60 (Yellow — "interesting but needs work"). The high score came from Distribution (App Store = built-in audience) and Moat (Vision AI + nutrition logic + privacy positioning = hard to copy as a bundle).

What pushed it over the line: every lifter I talked to had the same problem. "I know my sets. I have no idea about my food."

That's the validation gate. If 4/5 people you interview describe the pain without you prompting, you have something.
